1. The design this paper examines

A great deal of applied cheminformatics begins with a target and a question of the form: what kind of chemistry inhibits this protein? The standard way to answer it with public data has a recurring shape. You assemble every compound that has been reported in an assay against the target. You then place that collection alongside one or more reference libraries chosen to represent other regions of chemical matter: a pharmacological library of compounds with a clinical history, a natural-product collection, a toxicological inventory. You compute molecular descriptors for everything, project the descriptors or fingerprints into two dimensions, train a classifier to tell the groups apart, and report how well it does.

The result is then read as a statement about the target. If the classifier separates the inhibitors from the toxicological inventory, the inhibitors are said to occupy a region of chemical space distinct from toxicologically flagged matter. If the inhibitors sit visually close to the drug library, they are said to be drug-like. The paper under study quotes the conclusion in the form it usually takes: that the inhibitors of a target occupy a drug-like region of chemical space and remain separated from toxicological chemical space[1]. For urease specifically, an earlier and carefully executed survey of 3,200 compounds tested against the enzyme established the descriptive picture on which much of this reasoning rests[6].

That conclusion matters because it is actionable. It is cited to justify prioritising one series over another, to claim novelty for a scaffold, and to argue that a compound class is unlikely to carry toxicological liabilities. Those are decisions with costs attached.

Key idea — the shape of the claim

A cross-collection comparison measures how separable two labelled groups of molecules are given a molecular representation. It is interpreted as how chemically different the two kinds of molecule are. These are the same statement only if the group labels differ in nothing except chemistry. The whole paper is an investigation of what else the labels differ in.

The investigation is methodological, not biological. The paper does not claim that urease inhibitors are indistinguishable from environmental chemicals. It claims that the standard design cannot tell you whether they are, because a second variable is attached to both the group labels and the molecular descriptors. That variable is the subject of the next section.

2. Confounding

2.1 The definition

A confounder is a variable that is associated with both the grouping variable and the outcome variable, and that is not on the causal path from one to the other. When a confounder is present and unaccounted for, an observed difference between groups cannot be assigned to the cause of interest, because part or all of it may be produced by the confounder.

Written as a causal diagram, the situation that concerns us has three nodes. The grouping variable is collection membership — is this molecule in the urease set or in the comparison library? The outcome, in the statistical sense of the quantity being modelled, is the molecular representation: descriptors, fingerprints, whatever the classifier sees. The third node is provenance: which database deposited the record, which publication reported it, which laboratory produced it, in which year, against which organism.

Provenance points an arrow at collection membership, trivially and by construction: a molecule is in the urease set because some laboratory published a urease assay for it, and in the drug library because a curator judged it to have a clinical history. Provenance also points an arrow at the molecular representation, because a single medicinal-chemistry publication typically reports one congeneric series — twenty compounds built on one scaffold, varying at one or two positions, with similar molecular weight, similar ring counts and similar polarity. The arrow of interest, from collection membership to the molecular representation, is the target-related chemistry the study wants to measure. It is the arrow that provenance makes unidentifiable.

2.2 The mechanism on a synthetic dataset

Before touching the paper's molecules, we isolate the mechanism on data where the truth is known by construction. The following table is synthetic and labelled as such: it contains no real compounds. Two hypothetical collections, A and B, are assembled. By construction there is no chemical difference between them: the collections impose nothing at all on the molecules. The only structure is that collection A was assembled mostly from the output of Lab-1 and collection B mostly from Lab-2, and that the two laboratories happen to work on different molecular weight ranges — 280 against 420 g/mol, which is an entirely ordinary difference between a fragment-screening group and a group making extended amides.

# SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE - no real molecules. Collection has no effect on MolWt by construction;
# the only structure is that each collection draws mostly from one laboratory.
rng = np.random.default_rng(RANDOM_STATE)
n = 300
lab_A = rng.random(n) < 0.85          # collection A: 85 % of records come from Lab-1
lab_B = rng.random(n) < 0.15          # collection B: 15 % of records come from Lab-1

Now measure the separation the way a cross-collection comparison would, using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC AUC) of molecular weight as a predictor of collection membership. An AUC of 0.5 means the predictor is uninformative; 1.0 means perfect separation. The AUC has a direct interpretation worth memorising: it is the probability that a randomly chosen member of the positive class scores higher than a randomly chosen member of the negative class.

Pooled over both laboratories, molecular weight separates the two collections with an AUC of 0.860. Stratified by laboratory it separates them with 0.525 and 0.499 — that is, not at all. The pooled value is not a measurement error and not noise. It is a correct measurement of a real association between molecular weight and collection label; it is simply not a measurement of what the analyst wanted. Had these been real libraries, the analyst would have reported "the two collections are readily separated, AUC 0.86, driven by molecular weight", and that sentence would have been literally true and scientifically empty.

Two features of this example deserve emphasis because they recur in the real analysis. First, the confounder does not need to be perfectly associated with the grouping: 85:15 is enough to generate an AUC of 0.86. Second, conditioning fixes it here because we knew the confounder and recorded it. In the real study, the analogue of the lab column is the source publication, which is recorded for most but not all molecules, and the analogue of MolWt is twenty-five descriptors rather than one.

Pitfall — "the classifier found a real signal"

It did. A confounded result is not a false result; it is a true answer to a question nobody asked. This is why "the model achieved AUC 0.95" is not, by itself, evidence for any chemical or biological statement, and why the response to a high AUC should be what, concretely, could the model be keying on? rather than satisfaction.

2.3 The confounder of this study

In the paper, the confounder is library provenance, and specifically the publication-and-deposition process that put each molecule into each collection. The comparison groups are UI-Ref — the curated urease set — against CLUE, COCONUT and DSSTox. Those four groups were assembled by four entirely different processes: UI-Ref by abstracting medicinal-chemistry papers that happened to report a urease assay, CLUE by curating compounds with a clinical history and confirming the identity and purity of each physical sample[10], COCONUT by aggregating natural-product structures from open sources[11], and DSSTox by indexing substances of environmental and regulatory interest with assigned quality-control levels[12],[13]. Collection membership therefore encodes "which assembly process captured this molecule" at least as strongly as it encodes "what this molecule does to urease".

The paper's central empirical move is to measure the strength of the confounder directly rather than argue about it. If provenance structure alone can separate molecules, then the magnitude of that separation is the scale against which a cross-collection AUC should be read. Measured inside UI-Ref — where every molecule is a urease-assayed compound, so collection membership is held constant — a random forest on 25 physicochemical descriptors predicted the depositing database with an AUC of 0.954 (95% CI 0.939–0.968) and the source publication with a median one-versus-rest AUC of 0.997[1]. Notebook 2 of this series reproduces that experiment from scratch. For now, hold on to the number: within a single target-focused collection, basic molecular properties recover where a molecule was deposited almost perfectly.

3. Library provenance

3.1 What a collection is a sample of

Every public compound collection is a sample, and the sampling process is not chemistry. Four pieces of provenance metadata matter for this study, and it is worth naming them separately because they fail in different ways.

The depositing database is the resource from which the record was retrieved: ChEMBL, BindingDB or PubChem BioAssay for the urease records. These three overlap heavily but not completely, and each applies its own curation pipeline before publishing a structure. ChEMBL abstracts medicinal-chemistry papers manually and distributes standardised parent structures[7]; BindingDB curates measured affinities with target and ligand identity resolution[8]; PubChem aggregates depositor-submitted substances into compound records[9]. A molecule found only in BindingDB is not chemically different from one found only in PubChem — but it passed through a different pipeline, was reported by a partly different community, and may therefore differ systematically in size, polarity and scaffold.

The source publication is the document the measurement came from, identified by DOI, PubMed identifier or ChEMBL document identifier. This is the single most informative provenance field, because a publication is the unit in which medicinal chemistry is actually produced: one group, one scaffold, one assay protocol, one organism, one measurement batch.

The assay organism is the species whose urease was used. Jack-bean urease from Canavalia ensiformis is the cheap and widely used reagent; H. pylori urease is the therapeutically relevant one; Sporosarcina pasteurii and Proteus mirabilis ureases appear in subsets of the literature. The same compound can have very different apparent potency against different ureases, so organism is both a provenance field and an experimental covariate.

The endpoint type is what was measured: a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50), an inhibition constant (Ki), a dissociation constant (Kd), a half-maximal effective concentration (EC50), a percent inhibition at a single dose, or a qualitative active/inactive call. These are not interchangeable quantities, and only some of them can be converted to a comparable potency scale.

3.2 Why provenance is not a property of the molecule

A molecule has a molecular weight. It does not have a depositing database. Thiourea in BindingDB and thiourea in PubChem are the same substance; the difference between the two records is bibliographic. It is therefore uncomfortable to find that a model can recover provenance from structure alone — and the discomfort is informative. It means the sampling process left a structural fingerprint on the collection: not on any individual molecule, but on the set.

The paper makes this concrete with the observation that a classifier trained to separate a target's inhibitors from a reference library "can therefore solve the easier task of recognizing deposition provenance, whereas the conventional design cannot determine whether classification is based on provenance rather than target-related chemistry"[1].

We can see the first layer of this structure in the data directly. Molecules that appear in more than one database are, by definition, the ones that two independent curation pipelines both captured.

Bridge — to the biological-databases unit

The same reasoning applies to sequence and expression data. A set of genes assembled from "papers that studied gene X" carries the publication habits of that field; an expression dataset assembled from GEO carries the platform, the batch and the submitting laboratory. Provenance confounding is not a cheminformatics problem that happens to appear in bioinformatics. It is a property of assembling datasets from literature, and the arrow diagram of Figure 1 transfers unchanged.

4. Urease, in one page

Urease (urea amidohydrolase, EC 3.5.1.5) is a nickel-dependent metalloenzyme that hydrolyses urea to ammonia and carbamate; the carbamate then decomposes spontaneously to a second ammonia and carbon dioxide[3]. Its active site contains two nickel ions bridged by a carbamylated lysine, and a hydroxide ion bridging the two metals performs the nucleophilic attack on the urea carbonyl. That dinuclear nickel centre is the structural reason why so many reported inhibitors are metal chelators — hydroxamates, thioureas, catechols, phosphonates and organoselenium compounds all have a plausible route to coordinating the nickel pair, and Notebook 1 of this series counts how prevalent those motifs are in the reference set.

The enzyme matters medically because the ammonia it liberates raises local pH. In Helicobacter pylori, that local pH buffering is what permits colonisation of the gastric mucosa, and urease is accordingly a virulence factor and a drug target[2]. In Proteus mirabilis urinary infection, urease-driven alkalinisation precipitates magnesium ammonium phosphate — struvite — producing the infection stones that encrust urinary catheters[4]. Urease is also of agronomic interest, because it drives the loss of urea fertiliser as volatile ammonia[3].

The clinical precedent for inhibition is acetohydroxamic acid, a small hydroxamic acid approved for the management of infection-induced struvite stones, whose use is restricted by its tolerability[5]. Several decades of medicinal chemistry since then have produced hydroxamates, thioureas, polyphenols and organophosphorus scaffolds without delivering a second approved agent[5], and natural products have been surveyed extensively as an alternative source[19]. That history is why there is a large, scattered public record of urease assays to curate in the first place — and why the record is dominated by a relatively small number of chemotypes reported by a relatively small number of groups.

That is as much enzymology as the paper requires. The study is not about the mechanism of inhibition; it is about what can and cannot be inferred from the accumulated record of reported inhibition.

6. Assay record against unique molecule

6.1 Two counting units

The single most consequential decision in curating a bioactivity dataset is what one row means. There are two defensible units and they give different answers to almost every question.

An assay record is one measurement reported in one publication: this compound, this enzyme preparation, this endpoint, this number, this document. A unique molecule is a chemical structure, identified after standardisation by its InChIKey, which may carry one record or many. The relationship between them is many-to-one, and it is strongly uneven.

Key idea — declare the unit before you count

Record-level and molecule-level analyses answer different questions. Record level answers "what has the literature measured?"; molecule level answers "what chemical matter has been tested?". Mixing them — filtering at record level and reporting at molecule level without saying so — is the most common way a curation cascade becomes irreproducible.

Pitfall — filtering on the outcome variable

7. Reported activity is not potency

UI-Ref is a reported-activity set. Membership means a molecule was tested against urease and the result was published — not that it inhibited the enzyme usefully. The paper is explicit: "Inclusion indicates that a molecule was tested and reported, not that it exhibited sufficient potency to be of practical interest"[1].

For the 1,110 molecules with a direct quantitative endpoint, potency is expressed as $$\mathrm{pAct} = -\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\text{IC}_{50}, K_i, K_d \text{ or EC}_{50} \text{ in mol L}^{-1}}{1\ \text{mol L}^{-1}}\right) = 9 - \log_{10}(\text{value in nM}),$$ so that larger values mean greater apparent potency and one unit of pAct is one order of magnitude in concentration. Only IC50, Ki, Kd and EC50 values reported in nanomolar units were converted; minimum inhibitory concentrations, percent inhibition and inconsistent units were retained with flags but excluded from potency modelling[1]. Because values are aggregated per molecule across replicate records, each molecule carries a median, a minimum, a maximum and a spread.

8.2 Source publication almost determines the assay conditions

The sharper statement is that knowing the document tells you almost everything about the experimental conditions. The paper quantifies this with the normalised conditional entropy. For a variable $Y$ (say, assay organism) and a grouping $G$ (source document),

$$H(Y) = -\sum_y p(y)\log_2 p(y), \qquad H(Y \mid G) = \sum_g p(g) \left[-\sum_y p(y \mid g)\log_2 p(y \mid g)\right],$$

and the reported quantity is the ratio $H(Y \mid G)/H(Y)$. It is 1 when the grouping tells you nothing about $Y$, and 0 when the grouping determines $Y$ completely. One minus the ratio is the fraction of the variation in $Y$ that the grouping accounts for.

For the paper's claim to be wrong, this ratio would have to be close to 1 — documents would have to be spread across organisms, scaffolds and endpoint types, so that grouping validation folds by document would not also be grouping them by experimental condition.

def normalised_conditional_entropy(df, y, g):
    # H(Y|G) / H(Y) in bits. 0 = G determines Y completely; 1 = G says nothing about Y.
    sub = df.dropna(subset=[y, g])
    def H(p):
        p = p[p > 0]
        return float(-(p * np.log2(p)).sum())
    h_y = H(sub[y].value_counts(normalize=True).values)
    h_y_given_g = sum(len(blk) / len(sub) * H(blk[y].value_counts(normalize=True).values)
                      for _, blk in sub.groupby(g))
    return h_y_given_g / h_y, h_y

Source publication accounts for 98.2% of the variation in assay organism, 80.2% of the variation in scaffold class and 85.4% of the variation in endpoint type, and only 3 of the 77 named documents report assays against more than one organism. The paper reports 98.4%, 81.8% and 85.9% from the same data[1]; our values differ in the first decimal because the entropy is computed here over the 1,142 documented molecules with the scaffold field taken as published, whereas the paper's sheet S36 assigns singleton provenance groups to undocumented molecules. The conclusion is identical and robust to the choice: document is very nearly a proxy for the entire experimental context.

This is why the paper insists that validation folds be grouped by publication. Grouping by document is not a conservative statistical nicety; it is the only way to stop a model from being rewarded for recognising a laboratory's house style. And it has a cost that Notebook 3 quantifies: because document also determines organism, a document-grouped design cannot separate "generalises to a new publication" from "generalises to a new enzyme preparation". The two factors are not identified separately in this dataset, and no amount of modelling fixes that.

9. Where this series goes next

The remaining five notebooks follow the paper's argument in order. Each one takes a single methodological idea, teaches it from first principles on these same molecules, and then reproduces the corresponding result.

Notebook 1 — Cheminformatics foundations. Everything the later notebooks compute on molecules: how a SMILES string encodes a graph, why standardisation changes molecular identity and what it does to a database lookup, what each of the 25 physicochemical descriptors measures physically, how a Morgan/ECFP4 fingerprint hashes circular substructures into bits, how Tanimoto similarity is calculated and what a similarity of 0.35 actually looks like, what a Bemis–Murcko scaffold is, and how to write a SMARTS pattern for a functional motif and validate it with positive and negative controls. The scaffold definition used throughout is the molecular framework of Bemis and Murcko[18].

Notebook 2 — The provenance experiment. The paper's central measurement, reproduced: train a random forest to predict the depositing database and the source publication within UI-Ref, where collection membership is held constant, and recover AUCs around 0.954 and 0.997. This notebook also teaches the random forest itself, cross-validation, and bootstrap confidence intervals for an AUC.

Notebook 3 — Validation design. Why a random train/test split overstates performance when the data are clustered by publication; how grouped cross-validation works; how near-duplicate leakage is measured; and how property matching constructs a comparison set. Neither idea is new — the virtual-screening community established that decoy construction determines apparent enrichment, which is why property-matched decoys exist[15], and molecular machine learning has documented repeatedly that random splits overstate generalisation relative to scaffold-based or temporal splits[16]. The notebook reproduces the fall of the cross-collection AUCs from 0.956–0.982 to 0.762–0.841 and shows what matching does and does not balance.

Notebook 4 — Null models. What a difference does and does not prove. Label permutation, the library-assembly null that uses only a molecule's position within its own library, and the paired bootstrap and DeLong comparisons between correlated ROC curves. This is where the paper's strongest negative result lives: a null that knows nothing about absolute chemistry outperforms the real model.

Notebook 5 — Applicability domain and conformal prediction. How a model can decline to answer: nearest-neighbour and Mahalanobis domain criteria, probability calibration, and conformal prediction sets. It reproduces the finding that 92.8–97.0% of each external library lies outside the UI-Ref applicability domain and that only 10.3% of an external screening cohort completes the cascade with an informative prediction. The underlying requirement — that a model declare the region of chemical space in which its predictions are interpolation rather than extrapolation — is a long-standing consensus item in the QSAR literature[17].

10. Summary

You should be able to explain the following without notes.

  • A cross-collection chemical-space comparison measures the separability of labelled groups under a molecular representation, and is routinely interpreted as a statement about a target's chemistry. The two coincide only if the labels differ in nothing but chemistry.
  • A confounder is associated with both the grouping and the outcome and is not on the causal path between them; its presence makes the effect of interest unidentifiable. A pooled association of AUC 0.86 can coexist with a within-stratum association of 0.50, as the synthetic example showed.
  • Provenance — depositing database, source publication, assay organism, endpoint type — is a property of a record, not of a molecule, yet it leaves a structural signature on a set of molecules, because publications report congeneric series.
  • Urease is a nickel-dependent urea amidohydrolase (EC 3.5.1.5) whose dinuclear nickel site explains the prevalence of chelating chemotypes among its reported inhibitors; acetohydroxamic acid is the single approved precedent.
  • An assay record and a unique molecule are different units of observation. UI-Ref is 1,218 molecules drawn from 2,879 curated records; the median molecule carries 2 records and the busiest carries 78.
  • The curation cascade, recomputed: 2,879 records and 1,361 molecules after standardisation; 2,567 records and 1,218 molecules after restricting to confirmed urease assays; 1,110 molecules with a direct quantitative endpoint.
  • Reported activity is not potency. The median UI-Ref molecule has pAct 4.72 (about 19 µM) and only 178 molecules reach pAct ≥ 6.
  • UI-Ref's provenance is concentrated: 1,142 documented molecules over 77 documents, 20 of which cover half the set, with source publication accounting for roughly 98% of the variation in assay organism.

11. Self-assessment

Q1. In one sentence, what does a confounder do to an estimated effect? It contributes to the observed association through a path other than the one of interest, so that the estimate mixes the two paths and the effect of interest cannot be recovered from the estimate alone.
Q2. The synthetic example had no chemical difference between collections, yet molecular weight predicted collection membership with AUC 0.860. Was the model wrong? No. The model correctly measured an association that exists in the data. The error would be in the interpretation — reading the AUC as evidence that the collections contain different kinds of chemistry, when the association runs entirely through the laboratory variable.
Q3. Why is "depositing database" not a property of a molecule? Because the same substance can be deposited in several resources or in none, and which resource holds it depends on who published the measurement and which curation pipeline captured it. It is a property of a bibliographic record. The uncomfortable finding of the paper is that it is nevertheless predictable from structure at the level of a whole collection.
Q4. UI-Ref contains 1,218 molecules but 2,879 records were curated. Where did the other records go? Nowhere — they were aggregated. Several records can describe the same structure (median 2, maximum 78), and 312 records were removed because their assay was not a urease assay. The cascade is 2,879 records / 1,361 molecules after standardisation, then 2,567 records / 1,218 molecules after the urease filter.
Q5. A colleague reports that their urease classifier reaches AUC 0.97 under five-fold cross-validation on UI-Ref. What is the first question you ask? How were the folds constructed? With 20 documents covering half the set and source publication accounting for ~98% of the variation in assay organism, random folds place analogues from the same paper in both training and test sets.
Q6. What is the difference between n_records and n_direct in the UI-Ref table? n_records counts all curated assay records for the molecule, including qualitative outcomes and non-convertible endpoints. n_direct counts only those with a direct quantitative endpoint (IC50, Ki, Kd or EC50 in nanomolar units) that could be converted to pAct. 1,110 molecules have n_direct greater than zero.
Q7. Read the cascade cell again. Why is 3,038 the only number in Figure 3 marked "cited"? Because the pooled primary exports are third-party data under licences that forbid redistribution, so the released table starts after standardisation at 2,879 records. Everything downstream of that point is recomputed from the file.
Q8. The normalised conditional entropy of organism given document is about 0.018. Express that in words, and say what the value would be if documents were uninformative. Knowing which publication a molecule came from removes about 98% of the uncertainty about which organism's urease it was tested against. If documents were uninformative the ratio would be approximately 1.
Q9. Why does the paper call the metadata-only null model "degenerate"? Because the external collections carry none of UI-Ref's record, assay, document, organism or endpoint fields, so the mere presence of those fields separates the classes perfectly. The model would achieve an AUC of 1.0 while learning nothing about molecules.
Q10. A dataset of 98 molecules with at least one measured-inactive record sounds usable as a negative class. Why is it not? Because 39 of them also carry an active record, leaving a smaller unambiguous set; and because those molecules come from very few publications, so a model separating them from actives can succeed by recognising publication origin instead of inactivity. The paper reports 59 unambiguously inactive molecules from six publications.
Q11. Which single column of uiref_analysis.parquet would you group validation folds by, and which column would you most like to have but do not? primary_document, because it is the unit of production of the data. What is missing is a provenance field for the external collections — CLUE, COCONUT and DSSTox carry no document identifier, so a document-grouped design can only group the UI-Ref side.
Q12. State the paper's conclusion in a form that does not overclaim. After property matching and publication-grouped validation, a detectable difference remains between UI-Ref and the external collections, but the design cannot attribute it to target-related chemistry rather than to residual provenance structure. The analysis identifies a difference that cannot be attributed; it does not demonstrate the absence of a difference.

Glossary

Assay record
One reported measurement: compound, endpoint, value, organism, document. The unit of observation of records_curated.parquet.
Unique molecule
A chemical structure identified after standardisation by its InChIKey. The unit of observation of uiref_analysis.parquet.
Provenance
The metadata describing where a record came from: depositing database, source publication, assay identifier, organism, endpoint type, year.
Confounder
A variable associated with both the grouping and the outcome, not lying on the causal path between them, whose presence makes the effect of interest unidentifiable.
UI-Ref
The curated urease reference set of this paper: 1,218 molecules reported in urease assays in ChEMBL, BindingDB or PubChem, with assay- and document-level provenance retained.
pAct
Negative base-10 logarithm of a molar IC50, Ki, Kd or EC50; equivalently 9 − log10(value in nM). Higher means more potent.
ROC AUC
Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve; the probability that a randomly chosen positive scores above a randomly chosen negative. 0.5 is uninformative.
Normalised conditional entropy
H(Y | G) / H(Y), the fraction of the uncertainty in Y that survives after conditioning on the grouping G. Zero means G determines Y.
Congeneric series
A set of compounds built on one scaffold with variation at one or two positions, typically the content of a single medicinal-chemistry publication.
Reference library
An external compound collection used as a comparison group — here CLUE (clinical history), COCONUT (natural products) and DSSTox (regulatory/environmental substances).
